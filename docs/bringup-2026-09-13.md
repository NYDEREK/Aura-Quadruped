## Wi-Fi transmit-power finding

At the ESP32 default 19.5 dBm transmit limit, starting station association caused the 3.3 V domain to drop: both ESP32 and CP2102N disappeared and the ESP32 restarted. The nearby router was measured at about -35 dBm, so firmware limits transmit power to 10 dBm. With that limit the board associates reliably, remains stable and reports roughly -33 dBm RSSI. This points to limited transient-current margin on the assembled board's 3.3 V supply or local decoupling. The software setting is a working mitigation; the supply should still be checked before relying on maximum RF output power.

## ICM-42688-P fault

Expected `WHO_AM_I`: `0x47`. Observed: `0xFF` in every probe:

- SPI mode 0 at 1 MHz
- SPI mode 0 at 100 kHz
- SPI mode 3 at 100 kHz

`0xFF` on all probes means the ESP32 does not receive driven data on MISO. Check these hardware points in order:

1. 3.3 V at ICM VDDIO pin 5 and VDD pin 8, and ground at pin 6.
2. Continuity from GPIO21 to ICM CS pin 12 and whether CS pulses low during boot.
3. Continuity from GPIO22 to SCLK pin 13, GPIO23 to SDI pin 14 and GPIO19 to SDO pin 1.
4. ICM orientation, solder joints and the fitted part number.

The firmware keeps the IMU unavailable and continues blinking the LED, reporting power and accepting read-only console commands.

## Retest after resoldering

At 14:47 on 2026-09-13 the board was reset and tested again without changing the firmware. All three SPI probes returned `WHO_AM_I=0x00`. The earlier value was `0xFF`, so the electrical state of MISO changed after resoldering, but the ICM still did not return a valid register value. A constant zero most strongly suggests MISO/SDO is shorted or held low, the ICM is unpowered, or CS/clock/data does not reach the fitted package.

An additional firmware was then flashed and verified. With SPI detached and ICM CS held high, GPIO19 read `1` with its internal pull-up and `0` with its internal pull-down. The MISO net is therefore high impedance and is not hard-shorted to ground or 3.3 V. This leaves an open SDO joint/trace, missing VDD/VDDIO, missing CS/SCLK/SDI at the package, or a damaged/unresponsive ICM as the remaining causes. Voltage and signal measurements at the ICM package are required to separate those cases.

## Independent software audit

The schematic, the ICM-42688-P data sheet and the ESP-IDF 6.1 SPI API were checked again independently:

- ESP32 GPIO19/21/22/23 connect to the schematic nets MISO/CS/SCLK/MOSI.
- Those nets connect to ICM pins 1/12/13/14: AP_SDO/AP_CS/AP_SCLK/AP_SDI.
- `WHO_AM_I` is register `0x75`; its SPI read address is `0xF5` and the expected value is `0x47`.
- The normal driver sends an 8-bit command followed by an 8-bit receive phase in SPI mode 0, MSB first, at 1 MHz. These settings satisfy the data sheet.

A second implementation then bypassed the ESP-IDF SPI driver and generated the complete transfer directly on GPIO at approximately 33 kHz. It returned:

- MISO floating: `0xFF`
- MISO with internal pull-up: `0xFF`
- MISO with internal pull-down: `0x00`

The received byte follows the ESP32 pull resistor instead of the expected sensor response. The ICM is therefore not driving SDO during an addressed transfer. This independently rules out the ESP-IDF transaction construction as the cause. It does not distinguish an unpowered/damaged ICM from an open input/control connection or an open SDO joint.

## ICM schematic audit

The ICM-42688-P section was compared pin by pin with the manufacturer's typical 4-wire SPI circuit. No required schematic correction was found:

- ICM SDO pin 1 connects to ESP32 GPIO19 (`MISO`).
- ICM CS pin 12 connects to ESP32 GPIO21 (`CS`) and has a 10 kΩ pull-up, keeping the device deselected while the ESP32 resets.
- ICM SCLK pin 13 connects to ESP32 GPIO22 (`SCLK`).
- ICM SDI pin 14 connects to ESP32 GPIO23 (`MOSI`).
- VDDIO pin 5 and VDD pin 8 connect to 3.3 V, which is within the specified 1.71–3.6 V range.
- VDD has 100 nF plus 2.2 µF bypass capacitors; VDDIO has 10 nF. These are the values in TDK's reference circuit.
- GNDIO pin 6 and reserved pin 7 connect to ground as required.
- INT2/FSYNC/CLKIN pin 9 is grounded, which is allowed when that function is unused.
- Reserved pins 2, 3 and 10 are left open, while reserved pin 11 is grounded; both treatments follow the data-sheet pin instructions.
- INT1 pin 4 has a 10 kΩ pull-up. It is unused by the present firmware and cannot explain a failed `WHO_AM_I` read.

The schematic PDF does not contain the PCB footprint or copper layout, so this audit cannot verify physical pad numbering, a mirrored footprint, package orientation, trace continuity, or placement of the bypass capacitors. Those remain the main design/assembly checks before fitting another device.

## Retest after fitting the replacement ICM

At 16:02 CEST on 2026-09-13, after the ICM was replaced/resoldered, the board was reset through the recovered CP2102N port at `/dev/cu.usbserial-10`. The result remained unchanged:

- SPI mode 0 at 1 MHz: `WHO_AM_I=0xFF`
- SPI mode 0 at 100 kHz: `WHO_AM_I=0xFF`
- SPI mode 3 at 100 kHz: `WHO_AM_I=0xFF`
- independent GPIO bit-bang: floating `0xFF`, pull-up `0xFF`, pull-down `0x00`
- GPIO19 bias test: pull-up reads 1 and pull-down reads 0, confirming a high-impedance MISO line

The ESP32 booted normally, GPIO33 blinked, the INA226 responded with manufacturer ID `0x5449` and die ID `0x2260`, and UART was stable. The replacement ICM still does not drive SDO. Since the same failure persists across two fitted devices and two independent SPI implementations, the most likely remaining fault is the PCB footprint/pad mapping, package orientation, or an open CS/SCLK/SDI/SDO connection at the ICM pads. Actual VDD and VDDIO voltage at pins 8 and 5 should also be measured directly at the package.
